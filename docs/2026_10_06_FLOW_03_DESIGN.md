# FLOW 03 — cancel, `within` → `TimedOut`, preemption: design note (v2, DESIGN AND PROBES ONLY)
Tickets `avra-8sb5.34.53.3`, `.24`, `.28`; base `0d261da`. Nothing here is built. `f:` is `runtime/avra_fiber.c`. Every number is MEASURED on the lane's Sprite (Linux 6.12.105-fly x86_64, 8 cpus, clang 22.1.2) by a script in `tools/flow_bench/probes/{cancel,preempt,aftercall_ir}/`, or marked ESTIMATE. A probe is a package checked OUTSIDE the tree (`cancel/run.sh`); pNN names its file.
## 1. Today (READ and RUN)
- A cancel is recorded and answered by the next park BEFORE it switches (f:1237-1240), so a request already stands until someone clears it. A task parked by sleep, join or a descriptor park is not woken by one (f:1497-1500, f:1375, f:1519, f:1381, f:1554). `avra_task_cancel` is a row with no evaluator arm and no Avra caller (`core/runtime_api.av:1702-1707`).
- `within` is a library component that answers its block as written (`std-time/src/time.av:58-74`); a deadline reaches code only as each waiting verb's own error (`std-net/src/net.av:226`, `:274`). RUN, both engines (p10): under `within ms(30)` a caught timeout is heard again by the next socket wait at once, a `sleep(ms(60))` after it takes its 60 ms, and an inner `within secs(5)` reports the OUTER deadline as its own — "the outer block went on to its end, true (70 ms under a 30 ms deadline)". p11: a child spawned under a `within` (it inherits the deadline, f:1352) and joined after it answers `timed out` as an ordinary value.
- A `return` and a `?` inside the block leave through it today (`std-time/src/tests/within/within.av:6-27`); `std-tls/src/tls.av:363-366` depends on the `?`.

WHAT C ASKS OF THE COMPILER — the grammar learns nothing (`expression = l:disjunction ( "catch" … )?`, `features/expr_spine/mod.av:53`; `.await` is a property row, `features/tasks/mod.av:33`, and a paren group mints no node, so `(t.await) catch` is the same subject):
- THE TYPER: `catch` reads its subject's sides from the subject's TYPE alone (`features/results/check.av:29`). It asks ONE verb instead — a `Result`'s sides, or the FAILURE VIEW the subject's own row declares: `PropertyRow` gains that column, `await` answers `(T, Cancelled)`. The pair is never interned as a `Result`, so a task answering nothing needs no `Result<void, …>`. The results feature still matches no tasks node.
- LOWERING: the same row lowers the caught form as a join that answers how the task ended; a bare `.await` joins and, on a cancelled task, cancels the awaiter with the same asker (it unwinds; its own watcher sees it).
- `match t.await { … }` is the `T`: only `catch` sees the cancel. `t.await?` is REFUSED with its own voice — a cancel already passes through; `catch` it or drop the `?`. `let v = t.await` then `v catch …` stays refused (p12, "this is `int`"): the cancel left at the await.
- COHERENT WITH D1: one law — `catch` recovers the failure its subject can have. A `within` OWNS its deadline, so its failure is a value (`Result<T, TimedOut>`: `match`, `?` and a binding all hold it). An await's cancel is someone else's, so it is a channel that passes through by default and `catch` is the one place it lands. WHAT C CANNOT SAY: the cancel as DATA beside the answer (`List<Result<T, Cancelled>>`); the arm answers a `T`. Nothing in the tree wants it today; B′'s property is how it would be said.
- `t.cancel()` is still wanted as the verb that cancels (p08: free).

WHERE A CANCEL IS SEEN AND NOT OWNED (a `t.await catch` in a task that is itself under the asker), THE REQUEST STANDS: nothing clears `cancel_by` but its asker's scope ending, so the next cancel point raises it again — the park already answers so (f:1240). D5 is what bounds "next" in time.
**D4 — no `cancellable` word (working decision).** No user program was found that needs one: cleanup is `defer`; "who cancelled it, and where" is the awaiter's `catch c`. THE ONE PROGRAM THAT NEEDS A LANDING INSIDE A FN IS `within`: its block stays inline (§1's `return` and `?`), and as a lambda it is refused twice (p06: "a `return` answers a fn, and there is none enclosing"; "`?` passes the failure to a caller, and a lambda promises none"). So `within` becomes the tasks feature's own expression — `"within" limit body`, a head that keeps its brace, as `if` and `while` do — and its landing is a block form no program can spell. `@std/time` keeps `Duration` and `TimedOut` and loses the component (`std-time/src/time.av:58-74`); `features/tasks/mod.av:7-8` says the opposite today. p09: `cancellable` stays an ordinary free name.
**D5 — G3 preemption** (`preempt/run.sh`; ns a turn, least of five, two rounds, plain → checked).

|---|---|---|

**PENDING THE OWNER, as the lead reframed it — "like Go".** One byte, `avra_tick`; the switch reads it instead of the clock, a `while` back-edge reads it, and its cold side fires due timers, checks the running task's own deadline, raises a standing cancel, and yields when others are ready.
- WHICH LOOPS: a `while` (its trip count is nobody's to know) is checked every turn — measured free at a nanosecond a turn. A `for` over a list or a range is finite and carries no check: that keeps the two loops that paid (+18–22%, ×3.5) untouched and needs no constant. WHAT THIS LEAVES OPEN, said plainly: a `for` whose body is long and never waits is not preempted until it ends, and recursion that never waits is bounded only by its stack.
- THE PERIOD IS A NAMED SETTING, `AVRA_TICK_US`, default 1000, read once where the runtime settles its other flags; no literal period stands in the scheduler. The measured costs above are at that default.
- BEHIND THE PLATFORM SEAM (footprint door D7: no thread and no thread-local in `runtime/*.c`; `runtime/avra_platform.h` is where the runtime reads its machine). The scheduler calls two verbs, `avra_tick_armed()` and `avra_tick_stood_down()`, and reads one byte. A hosted target implements them with a thread in its own object, outside `runtime/*.c`; a no-OS target implements them over a timer interrupt that stores the same byte; wasm implements neither and has no preemption (as it has no scheduler today). The object rides the scheduler's: a program that never spawns links neither.
- ARMED ONLY WHILE IT CAN MATTER: when a task starts running with others ready or a deadline standing; stood down as the scheduler enters its poller, so an idle process pays nothing (left armed it measured 15.8–16.4 ms a second). Re-armed in `avra_fiber_forked`: a forked child saw no tick.

## 3. Mechanism
- RUNTIME. `unwinding` becomes a global byte the switch swaps beside `avra_task_local` (`runtime/avra_runtime.h:28-30`, f:1074, f:1088), set where a park answers −1 to the running task. Sleep, join and the descriptor park lose their deafness and go through `park_begun`'s order (claim, then cancel, then deadline). A `within` gets an id; `cancel_by` names a scope or a task. A joined task says how it ended (`TASK_END`, f:218).
- IR. One new value read — the byte — because a row call costs a call (1.6 ns against none); it is the tick's read too. Everything after it is today's shapes: `IfStart`, `failing()` (`compiler/lower/walk.av:417`), `FnExit`; inside a landing, a `LoopJump` out of a one-turn loop, `defer`s run as `jumped` runs them (`walk.av:410`, `core/ir.av:173`). The test is emitted by the call verbs alone (`features/emit.av:126-152`).
- THE EVALUATOR runs the same instructions; a `Job` carries the two bits.
- `at` is one frame a fn the unwind passes, pushed by that fn's single cold exit, capped at 16 (spec §9).

## 5. Tests — one per observer row of spec §5.4, each failing first, eval == native
1. the cancelled task: its `defer`s and `errdefer`s run, nothing after its cancel point does. 2. seeing one: `t.await catch c -> …` runs its arm with `c.by` and `c.at`; in a task itself under the asker the next cancel point still unwinds. 3. `within`: `Err(TimedOut { after, at })` over a sleep, a join and a socket read, each; an inner `within … catch` does not see an outer deadline (D3's program, R1's output). 4. a joiner: a bare `t.await` of a cancelled task cancels the awaiter, whose own watcher catches it; a task answering nothing, caught as a statement; `t.await?` and `v catch` refused, each by its voice. 5–7, named here and owed where their subject lands: a stream's `.ended` (slice 5), a failing block listing the children it cancelled (slice 5), a queue's redelivery (slice 7). 8. the operator's listing is `.25`. Beside them: a claim made before a cancel is taken first (trap 2); a spinning task is cancelled by its deadline and a second task still runs (`.24`).
## 6. Risks
1. "A call through a register may reach anything" multiplies the fact by 3.4 on the server and by 95 on the compiler; a fn TYPE that says it waits is the remedy, and is not designed here. 2. A block answering nothing cannot be a `Result` side yet (`features/results/check.av:286`): `within d { sleep(x) }` is refused until that lands, and says so. 3. A `catch` arm does not hear the ok side's type, so the migration's fold needs a named fn (p03). 4. The tick makes a hosted process that spawns two threads; a fork must re-arm it; a long `for` that never waits is not preempted (D5). 5. The server numbers sit inside a ±4% spread; the published row needs more rounds or a quieter machine. 6. Where `Cancelled` and `TimedOut` live is not settled (`Frame` is `@std/errors`', `Duration` is `@std/time`'s). 7. Under C the same `.await` joins two ways by where it stands; a helper fn that awaits hides the cancel from its caller's `catch` — the cancel passes through the helper and the caller alike, by design, and the refusal for `v catch` says where to catch.
