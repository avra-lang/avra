The 0.99 span this handoff predicted is `Maison Aster`. The organization actually owed never appears above 0.0, so no fitted threshold and no label wording in the declared space reaches it — `gloss`, `first_sentence` and the bare model name give byte-identical results (right=35, absent=3, mistyped=1 for all three).

**The mechanism is contextual blindness, and it is maximal-confidence in both directions.** The same span, same label:

```
"Velluto Works is our supplier."    -> organization 0.998
"Velluto Works to Maison Aster"     -> organization: 'Maison Aster' 1.0, person: 'Velluto' 1.0
     (in the invoice header, 'Velluto' falls to 0.132 product / 0.031 person /
      0.025 organization -- under the fitted 0.25 span threshold either way)
```

So a `X to Y` construction makes the encoder read one organization as a person and lose the other, and a possessive bare first name reads as an organization. There is nothing left to tune here: the two candidate fixes are mechanisms, and both need the owner's call.

1. **Feed the store's known entity labels in as mention candidates** — a record the graph already holds is evidence the text names it. Cheapest and most precise, but the extractor hook is a pure function of the envelope (`score.py` passes no graph), so this needs the extractor to see the store or the envelope to carry candidates.
2. **A closed-choice verification over a cross-label candidate pool** — the encoder proposes spans at a *lower* probe threshold, the scorer picks which is a `<Model>`. In s13 `Velluto` is already a candidate at 0.132 (under `product`), so a cross-label pool reaches it where a per-label one cannot; s04's email address sits at 0.003 and is probably unreachable either way. Needs a new fitted parameter and gym span gold that does not exist yet.

## Biggest levers, in order
1. **Extraction entity pass.** Four misses, two mechanisms above. Not the anchor, not the threshold.
2. **Record-kind choice:** held-out accuracy is 0.638 when choosing among 17 kinds. The shortlist path is built: `record_shortlist_k` and `extract/fit_shortlist.py`. Recall@5 is 0.914, and the score/fit step is RUNNING as of this handoff (`bench/shortlist.scores.jsonl`, resumable; it appends and skips what it has). It needs all 391 rows × 12 asks = 4692 scores at about 1/s; when it reaches that count, `python3 graph/extract/fit_shortlist.py fit --write graph/extract/params.json` picks the winner. **The resident service holds `params.json` at import, so restart `cascade.py --serve` before measuring anything with a new winner.**
3. **A second identity defect, separate from the entity pass: a text field takes a value that is not its own.** `Product.code` (identity namespace `sku`) came out as `avery@customer.example` in s19 and as `R-88` in s28, and the bogus sku then makes each source mint a fresh Product instead of linking. The pairing comes from the encoder's own structure query (it returns `name` and `code` in one record), so the guard belongs at acceptance. Two shapes, and only the first is a law already: a value another record holds as a *declared identity* cannot also fill a field here (s03, s15); a value that is plainly another record's number (s28) needs a declared check that does not exist yet. 5 sites in the current results.
3. **Planner abstention:** 39 of 47 unanswerable questions are answered confidently. `agreement()` abstention is built and off (`43a02c35`, threshold fitted at 0.05). Measure it on `eval-v1`.
4. **Planner ranking by training:** the rejection sampler and LoRA trainer are built and tested on dev (`planner/train/sample.py`, `prepare.py`, `train.py`, `e19ae7ab`). It needs gym train worlds: `gym/out/train/*`, about 8 built and disjoint from eval. Train, then score paired on `eval-v1`. The earlier LoRA failed because its prompts were short 2-option ones that didn't match the real planner's; this sampler keeps the exact planner bytes.
